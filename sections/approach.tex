\section{Approach}\label{sec:approach}

Section~\ref{sec:formulation} posed three sub-problems and
Section~\ref{sec:coupled} showed they compose into one semi-Markov
program. This section describes how each policy is computed and how the
three exchange information at run time. The design principle is that no
policy solves the joint program: each optimizes its own sub-problem on
its own clock, and coupling is carried by four quantities passed between
them, namely the committed level $\FSL_d$ and the terminal targets
$\etarget{v}$ passed downward from the commitment policy, the
event-value dual $\muDR$ passed across from the charging policy to the
negotiation policy, and the surviving buffer together with the running
peak carried forward to the next day. Figure~\ref{fig:processflow}
depicts the three policies and these couplings.

\subsection{Day-Ahead Commitment ($\pidr$)}\label{sec:approach-dr}

The commitment policy chooses $\FSL_d$ at the end of day $d{-}1$ by
\emph{trying out its own consequences}: it does not evaluate a
candidate level in closed form but simulates \emph{delivering on the commitment} on day $d$ (the charging and discharging the building would actually perform to stay under the cap once real arrivals are known, the second-stage recourse in stochastic-programming terms) under that candidate on sampled days drawn from the forecast $\psihat$, then keeps the levels for which delivery meets the risk target $\varepsilon$ (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}). Because the delivery it simulates is exactly the charging policy of
Section~\ref{sec:approach-ch} run on sampled arrivals, the commitment
and the delivery on it share one model of the day; the commitment can be
made before its backing resource exists precisely because that resource
is simulated in the trial.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\pidr$: day-ahead commitment by simulated delivery}
\label{alg:dr}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{forecast $\psihat$; delivery risk level $\varepsilon$; commitment
depth $\lambda \in [0,1]$; sample count $N$; baseline peak $\Phist$;
per-vehicle usable maxima $\{E_{\max}(v)\}$; candidate FSL grid
$\mathcal{F} \subseteq [\,\underline{F}, \Phist]$}
\KwOut{committed level $\FSL_d$; terminal SoC targets $\{\etarget{v}\}$}
set $\etarget{v} \leftarrow E_{\max}(v)$ for all $v$
\tcp*{uniform target allocator (current implementation)}
Draw $N$ sampled days $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_N \sim \psihat$ (arrivals,
required SoC, off-site use)\;
\ForEach{candidate level $F \in \mathcal{F}$}{
  \ForEach{sampled day $\omega_i$}{
    simulate delivering on $F$: run $\pich$ (Alg.~\ref{alg:ch}) under
    cap $F$ with targets $\{\etarget{v}\}$ on sampled day $\omega_i$\;
    $x_i \leftarrow \mathbb{1}[\exists\, t \in \evwin{d}:\ \Ptot{t} > F]$
    \tcp*{joint window exceedance}
  }
  $\hat{p}(F) \leftarrow \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i x_i$
  \tcp*{estimated violation probability}
}
$\Omeps \leftarrow \{F \in \mathcal{F} : \hat{p}(F) \le \varepsilon\}$
\tcp*{$\varepsilon$-feasible commitment set}
$\FSL_d \leftarrow \Phist - \lambda\,\bigl(\Phist - \min \Omeps\bigr)$
\tcp*{commitment-depth interpolation}
\Return $\FSL_d,\ \{\etarget{v}\}$\;
\end{algorithm}

The exceedance event in Algorithm~\ref{alg:dr} is evaluated
\emph{jointly} over the window, as the probability that any within-window
step exceeds $F$, not as a per-step marginal; a level that passes every
marginal can still fail the joint test, and only the joint test matches
the delivery guarantee the building sells. The interpolation returns the
null commitment $\FSL_d = \Phist$ at $\lambda = 0$ and the deepest
$\varepsilon$-feasible level at $\lambda = 1$, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:newsvendor} identifies the risk-neutral anchor
within this family. The terminal targets $\etarget{v}$ set how much buffer the commitment layer asks each user to leave behind. The current implementation uses a uniform allocator, $\etarget{v} = E_{\max}(v)$, asking every vehicle to bank to its usable maximum; this is the most aggressive admissible target and is deliberately simple. A refinement that distributes only the buffer the commitment actually requires, in proportion to each user's forecast availability across the sampled days in which delivery succeeded, would reduce unnecessary banking without changing the interface to $\pich$; we treat it as future work and report results under the uniform allocator.

\subsection{Real-Time Charging ($\pich$)}\label{sec:approach-ch}

The charging policy acts every control step under a model-predictive
loop. At step $t$ it samples continuations of the arrival process,
solves a mixed-integer program over the remaining horizon with the
committed $\FSL_d$ fixed, applies the first action, and rolls forward;
the objective is \eqref{eq:chobj}, combining energy, demand, the
soft-cap penalty, degradation, and the terminal banking reward
$\Kbank{v}\bshort{v}$ that pulls departures toward $\etarget{v}$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\pich$: model-predictive charging with banking}
\label{alg:ch}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{committed level $\FSL_d$; terminal SoC targets $\{\etarget{v}\}$;
forecast $\psihat$; estimated peak (headroom boundary) $\Pmaxhat$;
running cycle peak $\pmaxpast$; state at $t$ (SoC, load, present EVs)}
\KwOut{control action at $t$; event-value dual estimate $\muDR$}
\For{each control step $t$}{
  sample $K$ arrival continuations from $\psihat$\;
  solve the fixed-$\FSL_d$ MILP \eqref{eq:chobj} over the horizon
  (CPLEX), with SoC, negotiated-floor \eqref{eq:floor}, rate, and
  soft-cap \eqref{eq:fslslack} constraints\;
  apply the first-step charge/discharge action\;
  bank excess only where $\Ptot{t} < \min(\Pmaxhat,\, \pmaxpast)$, pulled
  toward each $\etarget{v}$ \tcp*{headroom-push with target-pull}
  record the dual $\muDR$ of the FSL-delivery requirement\;
}
\Return action; $\muDR$\;
\end{algorithm}

Two components govern banking. The target-pull, through
$\Kbank{v}\bshort{v}$, states \emph{how much} buffer the commitment
layer wants accumulated; the headroom-push cap states \emph{when} accumulating it is admissible, admitting charge above immediate need only into capacity that lies below both the estimated peak $\Pmaxhat$ and the running cycle peak $\pmaxpast$, so banking never creates a new demand-charge peak. When no event is anticipated,
$\muDR = 0$ and $\Kbank{v} = 0$, and the composition reduces exactly to
the opportunistic headroom rule of P-V2B, which is the null-commitment
special case. The dual $\muDR$ on the FSL-delivery requirement is the shadow price of that requirement: the marginal improvement in the building's objective per additional unit of guaranteed deliverable buffer, and hence the value the banking price passes to the user. A banking offer is quoted at arrival, possibly days before an event, so $\muDR$ at quote time cannot come from that day's operation; it is the \emph{expected} shadow price of delivering on the anticipated event, evaluated under $\psihat$. It is zero when no event is anticipated and positive on pre-event days in proportion to the buffer the building forecasts it will need. Being a forecast quantity, $\muDR$ is the single price-forecast entering any user-facing offer, consistent with the incentive analysis of Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}.


\subsection{Per-Arrival Negotiation and Settlement ($\pineg$)}\label{sec:approach-neg}

The negotiation policy acts at each arrival and each departure. On
arrival it prices the flexibility menu and the banking option under the
committed-FSL objective and presents them alongside the reject-all
outside option; on departure it settles every user-facing term on
realized quantities through a single replay of the day.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{$\pineg$: negotiation at arrival, settlement at departure}
\label{alg:neg}
\DontPrintSemicolon
\KwIn{arrival report (required SoC $\Ereq$, requested departure
$\treq$); flexibility menu (option set offered to $v$); committed level
$\FSL_d$; event-value dual $\muDR$; yield estimate $\rhohat_v$; banking
prices (mean banked-energy price $\bar{w}^e_k$, forecast next-session
cost $\cmarg{v}$, demand-charge marginal $\DCmarg$); external rate
$\wext$}
\KwOut{accepted option or reject-all; user session cost $\ucost{k}{v}$}
\tcc{arrival}
compute the flexibility value $\Uflex$ for each menu option under
\eqref{eq:uflex}\;
form the banking price \eqref{eq:pbank}\;
\quad $\pbank{v}{k} = [\max(\bar{w}^e_k, \cmarg{v}) + \DCmarg - \muDR\rhohat_v]^+$\;
present menu $+$ banking price $+$ reject-all at $\wext$\;
user selects; if reject-all, charge externally at $\wext$ and exit\;
\tcc{departure}
re-solve day $d$ rates-only, with and without $v$'s surviving buffer
$\survived{v}{k}$\;
$\Udc(v,k) \leftarrow \wdr{}\,(\pmaxre(\!\setminus\!v) - \pmaxre)^+$\;
\quad\tcp*[h]{demand-charge share of the replay}\;
$\Ubuf(v,k) \leftarrow [\text{replay difference}] - \Udc(v,k)$\;
\quad\tcp*[h]{energy-plus-penalty share}\;
settle $\ucost{k}{v}$ \eqref{eq:usercost}, credits capped by the joint
replay saving\;
\Return selection; $\ucost{k}{v}$\;
\end{algorithm}

The banking price makes the over-charge free exactly when the building
values the buffer, through the $\muDR\rhohat_v$ term, and
arbitrage-neutral otherwise, through the $\max$; the derivation and its
incentive consequence are in Section~\ref{sec:formulation} and
Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}. The departure settlement re-solves the
realized day with rates as the only free variables, so the difference
between the with-buffer and without-buffer solves is the true marginal
value of $v$'s buffer; partitioning that one difference into $\Udc$ and
$\Ubuf$ attributes the demand-charge and the energy-plus-penalty shares
without paying any step twice, and the proportional cap of
Lemma~\ref{lem:replaybf} keeps the total within the realized saving.

\subsection{Coupling and Complexity}\label{sec:approach-coupling}

The four coupling quantities are the entire interface between policies.
Within a day the dependence is one-way: $\FSL_d$ and $\etarget{v}$
constrain and steer $\pich$, whose realized dispatch and dual $\muDR$
set what $\pineg$ can offer; nothing flows back within the day. Across days only the surviving buffer $\buff{d{+}1}$ and the running peak enter the next commitment. The terminal target $\etarget{v}$ is precisely the per-user scalar of Theorem~\ref{thm:decomp}'s policy class: carrying the commitment down into charging through this one number per user is what makes the per-day decomposition exact.
The dominant cost is the model-predictive charging solve: one
mixed-integer program per control step per sampled continuation, and
$N$ delivery simulations per candidate level in the day-ahead pass. Both
are parallel across sampled days, and the day-ahead pass is amortized over
the whole operating day; the settlement replay adds two rates-only
solves per departing user, reusing the same delivery model.
