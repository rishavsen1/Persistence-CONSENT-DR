\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:formulation}

\input{diagrams/process_flow}

We consider a single commercial building that manages its own EV charging infrastructure, serves a population of persistent EV users, and participates in a utility demand response (DR) program. The system performs three processes at three timescales, each governed by its own policy: (i) day-ahead, a commitment policy $\pidr$ commits a Firm Service Level to the utility; (ii) in real time, a charging policy $\pich$ schedules charging and discharging to deliver against the commitment at minimum cost; (iii) at each user arrival, a negotiation policy $\pineg$ offers compensated adjustments to the user's required SoC and departure time, and extends a cost-free over-charge offer when headroom permits. We first declare the shared physical layer (Section~\ref{sec:specs}); state the complete coupled problem and its ideal (oracle) solution, which the system as a whole approximates (Section~\ref{sec:coupled-ideal}); then close each of the three resulting sub-problems in turn with its definitions, constraints, and objective contribution (Sections~\ref{sec:dr}--\ref{sec:negotiation}); and finally record the information structure and feasible solution that the decomposition induces (Section~\ref{sec:coupled}). The complete variable dependency structure is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:vartree}.

\subsection{Specifications}\label{sec:specs}

\paragraph{Time structure.} The billing cycle consists of days $d \in \Dset$. Each day is discretized into time steps $t \in \Td$ of length $\dt$ (e.g., 15 minutes), and $\Tset = \cup_d \Td$. The demand charge is assessed over designated peak periods $\Tpeak \subseteq \Tset$. Control decisions are taken at the start of each step and held constant for its duration.

\paragraph{Persistent users and sessions.} Let $\Vset$ be the set of EV
users, each with a consistent identity across the billing cycle. User $v$'s visits are indexed by sessions $k \in \Kv$, with first session $k = 0$; session $k$ spans $[\tarr(v,k), \tdep(v,k)] \subseteq \Tset$, and $\Tvk$ denotes its time steps. Sessions need not occur on consecutive days; the formulation accommodates arbitrary gaps between visits. At plug-in, the arrival time $\tarr(v,k)$ and arrival SoC $\Earr(v,k)$ are observed, and the user reports a required departure SoC $\Ereq(v,k)$ and departure time $\treq(v,k)$. Let $\Vd{d} \subseteq \Vset$ denote the users with a session active on day $d$.

\paragraph{Battery state and persistence.} The SoC of EV $v$ at time $t$
is $\soc{t}{v}$, expressed in energy units (kWh) and bounded by the usable battery window:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:socbounds}
\Emin(v) \;\le\; \soc{t}{v} \;\le\; \Emax(v).
\end{equation}
Within a session, the SoC evolves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soc}
\soc{t+1}{v} \;=\; \soc{t}{v} + \rate{t}{v}\,\dt,
\end{equation}
where $\rate{t}{v}$ (kW) is the charging rate, negative for discharge; since at most one session is active at any time, the session index is omitted from per-step quantities. Between sessions $k$ and $k{+}1$, the EV consumes $\Eext(v,k)$ energy off-site (kWh), net of any external charging, with $\Eext(v,k) \le \Edep(v,k)$, so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:carryover}
\Earr(v,k{+}1) \;=\; \Edep(v,k) - \Eext(v,k),
\end{equation}
where $\Edep(v,k)$ is the SoC at departure from session $k$. The arrival SoC is therefore exogenous only at $k = 0$; for every later session it is determined by \eqref{eq:carryover}, which is precisely what makes the fleet an inter-temporally coupled resource and what single-day formulations cannot represent.

\paragraph{Chargers and assignment.} The building operates a set
$\mathcal{C}$ of heterogeneous charger types with $N_c$ units of type $c$, rate limits $[\rmin{c}, \rmax{c}]$ ($\rmin{c} < 0$ only for bidirectional chargers), and charging/discharging efficiency $\eta$. An assignment map $\assign_t : \mathcal{V}_t \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C}_t$ matches present EVs to chargers one-to-one, first-come, first-served, fixed for the duration of the session; an EV that arrives when no charger is free cannot participate. The availability indicator is $\avail{t}{v}
\in \{0,1\}$. Charging-rate slowdown at high SoC is modeled by
piecewise-linear segments and deferred to the appendix.

\paragraph{Building power and electricity cost.} The building's total
power draw is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:load}
\Ptot{t} \;=\; \bload{t} + \sum_{v \in \Vset} \avail{t}{v}\,\rate{t}{v},
\qquad \Ptot{t} \ge 0,
\end{equation}
where $\bload{t}$ (kW) is the non-EV building load. The energy cost at step $t$ is $g_t = \we{t}\,\Ptot{t}\,\dt$ with time-of-use price $\we{t}$ (\$/kWh), and the energy cost attributed to user $v$ sums the energy $\gch{t}{v}$ delivered to their EV ($\gch{t}{v} < 0$ when discharging). The demand cost is $\wdr{}\cdot\pmax$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peak}
\pmax \;=\; \max\Bigl(\pmaxpast,\; \max_{t \in \Tpeak} \Ptot{t}\Bigr)
\end{equation}
and $\pmaxpast$ is the running peak realized so far in the billing cycle, carried as state because persistent EVs couple consecutive days. Discharging accrues a degradation cost $\Kbatt$ per kWh, tracked by $\qdis{t}{v} \ge -\rate{t}{v}\dt$, and departing below the negotiated floor incurs $\Ksoc$ per kWh of shortfall $\zund{k}{v}$.

\paragraph{Uncertainty model.} All stochasticity is collected in a
single joint law $\psi$ over: the non-EV load $\{\bload{t}\}$; the future-user process (session occurrence and arrival times of not-yet-arrived users, their requests, option choices, and over-charge acceptances); the off-site usage $\{\Eext(v,k)\}$; and the utility's event calls $\{\evwin{d}\}$. Every expectation in this paper is taken with respect to $\psi$ unless stated otherwise.

\paragraph{Forecast models.} The controller does not know $\psi$ and
operates with a learned approximation $\psihat$: a load forecast $\hat{b}_t$; a generative user-forecast model producing future arrivals, requests, option choices, and over-charge acceptances, whose acceptance rate we denote $\ohat$; a yield estimate $\rhohat$ fitted to realized survival history; and an event-likelihood model for $\evwin{d}$. Two derived forecasts recur: the forecast realizable buffer $\buffhat{d}$ (Section~\ref{sec:charging}) and the estimated peak $\Pmaxhat$, which serves as the headroom boundary for proactive over-charging.

\paragraph{Policies.} The building's policy is the triple $\pi =
(\pidr, \pich, \pineg)$ with decision epochs at the end of each day, at every time step, and at every user arrival, respectively. All derived quantities below (SoC trajectories, peaks, costs) are implicitly determined by $\pi$ and the realized user choices; we omit this dependence from the notation for readability.


\subsection{The Coupled Problem and Its Ideal Solution}\label{sec:coupled-ideal}

Before decomposing the system into the three policies above, we state the
problem they jointly approximate. This serves two purposes: it makes the
coupling between demand response, persistence, and negotiation explicit,
and it furnishes an unattainable benchmark against which any tractable
policy can be measured.

\paragraph{A mechanism-design problem, not a control problem.} The
building does not schedule obedient batteries; it elicits flexibility from
autonomous, self-interested users who hold private information (their true
required SoC and inconvenience of deviation) and who may charge elsewhere
if the terms are unfavorable. The complete problem therefore minimizes the
building's net cost \emph{inclusive of the payments it makes to users},
subject to delivering its grid commitment, and subject to each user being
truthfully served, individually rational, and budget-feasible to
compensate. Payments are not an accounting overlay added after control;
they are decision variables inside the objective, and the constraints that
govern them are part of the problem statement.

\paragraph{The sequential problem.} Decisions arrive at three interleaved
timescales over the billing cycle and must be taken without knowledge of
what has not yet occurred. Let the system state at time $t$ be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state}
\State_t = \bigl(\{\soc{t}{v}\}_{v\in\Vset_t},\ \assign_t,\
\pmaxpast,\ \FSL_d,\ \bload{t}\bigr),
\end{equation}
collecting the SoC of connected EVs, charger occupancy, the running
billing-cycle peak, the day's committed FSL, and the building load;
$\pmaxpast$ and the persistence buffer it summarizes are the components
that couple consecutive days. Writing $\Filt_t$ for the information
available at $t$, the building seeks policies
$\pi=(\pidr,\pich,\pineg)$ solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bellman}
\Vfn(\State_t) = \min_{a_t\ \in\ \mathcal{A}(\State_t),\ a_t\,\preceq\,\Filt_t}\
\mathbb{E}_{\psi}\bigl[\, c(\State_t,a_t) + \Vfn(\State_{t+1}) \,\bigr],
\end{equation}
where the action $a_t$ ranges over the commitment, charging, and
negotiation decisions active at $t$; $c(\cdot)$ is the corresponding
increment of the net cost \eqref{eq:J}; the constraint $a_t\preceq\Filt_t$
enforces non-anticipativity (each decision depends only on what is known
when it is made); and the expectation is over the uncertainty law $\psi$.
Equation \eqref{eq:bellman} is the problem we would ideally solve: it
optimizes the commitment, the charging, and the negotiated agreements
\emph{jointly}, respecting both the gradual revelation of information and
the users' private types. It is intractable for the same two reasons it is
faithful, namely the curse of dimensionality in $\State_t$ and the private,
strategic nature of the users encoded in $\psi$.

\paragraph{The oracle.} Relaxing \emph{information alone} yields a
benchmark we can state in closed form. Suppose a planner observes the
entire realization in advance, every arrival, off-site trip, event call,
and load value, and knows each user's true type. Every expectation in
\eqref{eq:bellman} then collapses onto a known quantity and every
non-anticipativity constraint disappears, and the recursion degenerates
into a single full-horizon optimization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle}
\Porc:\quad \min_{\{\FSL_d\},\,\{\rate{t}{v}\},\,\{\Eneg,\tdep\},\,\{\text{payments}\}}\ J
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \eqref{eq:socbounds}\text{--}\eqref{eq:buffer},\ \text{IR, BF},
\end{equation}
with $J$ exactly as in \eqref{eq:J}. Crucially, the oracle relaxes
information, not autonomy: users remain free agents, so each must still be
left no worse off than charging externally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ir}
\ucost{k}{v}\ \le\ \zext(v,k)\qquad\text{(individual rationality, IR)},
\end{equation}
and the building still pays for the flexibility it uses, with each payment
bounded by the cost that user demonstrably saves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf}
\ucost{k}{v}\ \ge\ -\,\Uexact(\theta_v)\qquad\text{(budget feasibility, BF)},
\end{equation}
where $\Uexact(\theta_v)$ is the user's \emph{exact} marginal value, the
difference in $J$ between the optimal plan with and without $v$'s agreed
flexibility, computable directly because types are known. Because types
are known, truthful elicitation is free and strategy-proofness imposes no
constraint; the planner sets each user's agreed point $(\Eneg,\tdep)$
directly. The oracle is unattainable in deployment, but $\Porc$ is a valid
lower bound on the net cost of any policy, and it optimizes the
\emph{same} objective \eqref{eq:J} as the deployed system, so the two
differ only in information and decomposition, not in what they value.

\paragraph{Three coupling channels.} The oracle sees, and trades off at
once, three distinct couplings that any decomposition must contend with:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Intra-day, commitment to delivery to pricing.} A deeper
$\FSL_d$ tightens the charging feasible set \eqref{eq:fslslack}, which
determines the discharge the negotiation can offer and the value at which
flexibility prices out. This chain runs in one direction within a day.
\item \textbf{Inter-day, through the buffer.} Energy banked and survived
on one visit \eqref{eq:banked}--\eqref{eq:survived} is the buffer
\eqref{eq:buffer} deliverable on the next, which sets how deep a future
commitment can safely go (Proposition~\ref{prop:monotone}). This is the
carryover \eqref{eq:carryover} lifted to the planning level.
\item \textbf{Inter-day, through the peak.} The demand charge is a maximum
over the entire cycle \eqref{eq:peak}, so a charging choice on one day can
be rendered worthless or decisive by a peak set on another. Persistence
breaks the per-day separability that a single-building, non-persistent
controller could exploit, so this coupling cannot be removed by treating
days independently.
\end{enumerate}
The oracle resolves all three simultaneously. The decomposition we develop
next severs the intra-day chain into a fixed one-way order and approximates
the two inter-day couplings through the forecasts $\psihat$; it does not
alter the objective, the payments, or the constraints, only the order and
information under which they are solved.

\paragraph{From the oracle to three sub-problems.} Restoring sequential,
private information forces a split into the three policies of
Sections~\ref{sec:dr}--\ref{sec:negotiation}: a day-ahead commitment
problem $\Pdr$, a real-time charging problem $\Pch$, and a per-arrival
negotiation problem $\Pneg$. The split is a tractability device, not a
change of problem: $\Pdr$, $\Pch$, and $\Pneg$ together optimize the same
$J$ under the same IR and BF constraints as $\Porc$. What the
decomposition adds is exactly what private, sequential information costs:
incentive compatibility (strategy-proofness, the price of not knowing
types), a per-arrival options menu and cost-sharing parameter $\alpha$
(the mechanism that elicits flexibility from autonomous users in real
time), and a counterfactual-replay estimator of $\Uexact$ (a tractable
proxy for the exact marginal value the oracle computes directly). The gap
between $\Porc$ and the decomposed policy is therefore the combined price
of these three concessions, and we quantify it empirically in Section~7.

\paragraph{Alternative: a social objective.} The formulation above prices
flexibility through participation constraints: a user's inconvenience
enters only as the threshold \eqref{eq:ir} above which they must be
compensated. A natural upgrade folds inconvenience directly into the
objective, minimizing building cost \emph{plus} total user inconvenience
$\sum_{v,k} I^v_k$ rather than treating it as a constraint. Under this
social formulation the participation constraint relaxes, since a user may
be assigned a deviation whose inconvenience exceeds their compensation if
it improves total welfare, and the payments become the mechanism that
splits the joint surplus rather than transfers bounded by individual
savings. The social optimum can incur higher building cost yet higher
total welfare than $\Porc$. We adopt the building-cost objective with
user constraints in what follows, as it matches the cost accounting of the
prior systems and keeps the survey-estimated inconvenience weights out of
the objective; we return to the social formulation as a direction for
extension.

\subsection{Process I: Demand-Response Commitment}\label{sec:dr}

\paragraph{Commitment.} At the end of day $d{-}1$, the commitment policy
$\pidr$ selects a Firm Service Level $\FSL_d$ (kW); no other decision is taken at this layer. The committed FSL is non-anticipative: it is a function only of information available a day ahead, namely $\psihat$ and the state carried from prior days, and may not depend on any quantity realized during day $d$.

\paragraph{Payment and penalty.} The utility registers a baseline
$\Phist$ (kW) at enrollment from historical billing data and may call an event with window $\evwin{d} \subseteq \Td$, possibly empty. The capacity payment and the violation penalty are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dr}
\Cinc{d} = \winc\,(\Phist - \FSL_d), \qquad
\Cpen{d} = \Kfsl \sum_{t \in \evwin{d}} \vio{t}\,\dt,
\end{equation}
where $\winc$ (\$/kW) is the per-day-normalized capacity rate and $\vio{t} = \max(0,\, \Ptot{t} - \FSL_d)$ is the violation slack, defined only on $\evwin{d}$. The committed FSL is the only commitment in the system; no user-facing quantity is subject to commitment, penalty, or prepayment.

\paragraph{Feasible commitment set and commitment depth.} Delivery of a
committed FSL is a probabilistic statement under $\psihat$. The $\varepsilon$-feasible commitment set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pledgeset}
\Omeps(\buffhat{d}) = \Bigl\{ \FSL :
\Pr_{\psihat}\bigl(\Ptot{t} \le \FSL,\ \forall t \in \evwin{d}\bigr)
\ge 1 - \varepsilon \Bigr\},
\end{equation}
where the delivery risk level $\varepsilon$ is the operator's tolerance for violating the committed FSL during an event. The building commits at depth $\lambda \in [0,1]$ within this set:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pledgedepth}
\FSL_d(\lambda) \;=\; \Phist - \lambda\,\bigl(\Phist -
\min \Omeps(\buffhat{d})\bigr),
\end{equation}
interpolating between the null commitment $\FSL_d = \Phist$ (zero revenue, zero risk) and the deepest commitment feasible at risk level $\varepsilon$. The probability in \eqref{eq:pledgeset} is evaluated by sample average approximation: candidate values are scored by simulating the delivery of Process~II over scenarios drawn from $\psihat$, not in closed form. Because $\varepsilon$ hedges sampled randomness while $\lambda$ hedges error in $\psihat$ itself, the pair $(\lambda, \varepsilon)$ is treated as a first-class operating parameter and its revenue--penalty trade-off is characterized in Section~7.

The set $\Omeps$ formalizes the central structural claim of the architecture: a larger forecast buffer weakly enlarges the set of deliverable commitments, so persistence-aware over-charging expands the capacity the building can sell upstream.

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of the feasible commitment set]
\label{prop:monotone}
Fix the delivery risk level $\varepsilon$ and the forecast $\psihat$. If $\buffhat{d} \le \buffhat{d}'$ (a pointwise-larger forecast buffer, deliverable on the same chargers within the event window), then FSL commitment $\Omeps(\buffhat{d}) \subseteq \Omeps(\buffhat{d}')$. Consequently $\min \Omeps(\buffhat{d}') \le \min \Omeps(\buffhat{d})$: the highest deliverable commitment is weakly lower, and the capacity revenue $\Cinc{d}$ attainable at risk level $\varepsilon$ is weakly higher.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\textit{Proof sketch.} For any fixed scenario drawn from
$\psihat$, additional dischargeable energy can only relax the delivery constraint $\Ptot{t} \le \FSL$ on $\evwin{d}$: the controller retains every feasible charging plan it had with the smaller buffer and gains new ones, so the per-scenario minimum deliverable $\FSL$ is nonincreasing in the buffer. Taking the probability over scenarios preserves the inequality, hence the chance constraint
\eqref{eq:pledgeset} is satisfied by a superset of commitments. The
revenue statement follows from \eqref{eq:dr}, which is decreasing in $\FSL_d$. The argument assumes the extra buffer is dischargeable through bidirectional-assigned capacity within $\evwin{d}$; buffer stranded on unidirectional chargers does not enter $\buff{d}$ by construction
\eqref{eq:buffer}. A full proof, including the sample-average
approximation of \eqref{eq:pledgeset} and the charger-capacity edge cases, is deferred to Section~4. \hfill$\square$

\begin{remark}[Program stylization]\label{rem:program}
Deployed programs differ in commitment cadence: the Base Interruptible Program fixes the FSL at enrollment for a season, whereas capacity bidding programs admit nomination with day-ahead and day-of options. We model a day-ahead re-committable FSL, corresponding most closely to the day-ahead capacity-bidding option, and normalize the capacity rate to $\winc$ per committed day. An enrollment-fixed FSL is the constrained special case $\FSL_d \equiv \FSL$ and is reported in the sensitivity analysis.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Process II: Persistence-Aware Charging}\label{sec:charging}

\paragraph{Action and constraints.} At each step $t$, the charging
policy $\pich$ selects the rates $\act{t} = \{\rate{t}{v}\}$ subject to the SoC bounds \eqref{eq:socbounds}, the dynamics \eqref{eq:soc}, the rate limits of the assigned charger, and two service constraints. First, the negotiated floor is a guarantee at the negotiated departure:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:floor}
\Edep(v,k) \;\ge\; \Eneg(v,k),
\end{equation}
where $\Eneg(v,k) \le \Ereq(v,k)$ is set by Process~III. Second, during event windows the committed FSL enters as a soft-capped constraint,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fslslack}
\Ptot{t} \;\le\; \FSL_d + \vio{t}, \qquad t \in \evwin{d},
\end{equation}
with slack penalized at $\Kfsl$ through \eqref{eq:dr}. On event days the controller may discharge any EV down to $\Emin(v)$, drawing on energy above the floor, provided \eqref{eq:floor} is restored by the negotiated departure; the floor constrains the departure state, not the trajectory.

\paragraph{Over-charging into headroom.} On off-peak days the controller
may charge a consenting user above the negotiated floor, but only into available headroom: over-charge is permitted at step $t$ only while $\Ptot{t}$ remains below the estimated peak $\Pmaxhat$ (and below the event cap when one is active), so that banking energy never creates a new peak or an FSL violation. This is the proactive-charging mechanism of P-V2B, with the FSL acting as an additional cap.

\paragraph{Buffer accounting.} The banked excess at departure from
session $k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:banked}
\banked{v}{k} \;=\; \Edep(v,k) - \Eneg(v,k) \;\ge\; 0 \quad \text{(kWh)},
\end{equation}
nonnegative by \eqref{eq:floor}. The portion still present at the next arrival is the survived excess,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:survived}
\survived{v}{k{+}1} \;=\; \min\bigl(\banked{v}{k},\;
\Earr(v,k{+}1)\bigr) \quad \text{(kWh)}:
\end{equation}
the excess survives in full unless off-site consumption draws the battery below the banked amount, in which case only the energy present at arrival is attributable to the over-charge. The per-user yield is $\yieldf{v}{k{+}1} = \survived{v}{k{+}1}/\banked{v}{k}$ whenever $\banked{v}{k} > 0$, and the realizable buffer on day $d$ aggregates the survived excess of accepted offers,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:buffer}
\buff{d} \;=\; \sum_{v \in \Vd{d}} \accept{v}{k{-}1}\cdot
\survived{v}{k} \quad \text{(kWh)},
\end{equation}
where $k$ denotes $v$'s session on day $d$ and $\accept{v}{k{-}1}$ is the acceptance indicator of Section~\ref{sec:negotiation}. The population averages of yield and acceptance gate the value of the architecture: their product, scaled by the per-kWh value of event-window discharge, bounds the improvement over the non-committing system. Both are measured in Section~7; neither is assumed.

\begin{assumption}[Exogenous off-site usage]\label{as:exo}
$\Eext(v,k)$ is unaffected by the over-charge decision; cost-free energy does not induce additional vehicle usage. This is a behavioral assumption subject to a potential rebound effect, revisited when interpreting the measured yield (Section~7).
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Objective contribution.} Over its horizon, $\pich$ minimizes
the expected sum of energy cost, the demand-cost increment through
\eqref{eq:peak}, the floor and degradation penalties, and the FSL
penalty:
\begin{multline}\label{eq:chobj}
\min_{\{\act{t}\}} \;
\mathbb{E}_{\psihat}\Bigl[\sum_{t} g_t + \wdr{}\,\pmax
+ \Ksoc \sum_{v} \zund{k}{v} + \Kbatt \sum_{v,t} \qdis{t}{v}\\ + \Kfsl \sum_{t \in \evwin{d}} \vio{t}\,\dt \Bigr]
\end{multline}
subject to \eqref{eq:socbounds}--\eqref{eq:fslslack} and the headroom rule.

\subsection{Process III: User Negotiation}\label{sec:negotiation}

\paragraph{Choices and options.} User $v$'s choice $\theta_v$ records
the observed arrival data and the negotiated departure SoC and time. Flexibility is parameterized by levels $l \in \{0, \ldots, L\}$ with deviation bounds $\Delta e^{\max}_l$ and $\Delta t^{\max}_{\mathrm{dep},l}$; level $l = 0$ is the user's exact request. Upon each arrival, $\pineg$ offers one option per level, each a tuple of SoC deviation, departure deviation, and user cost $(\Delta e_l, \Delta t_{\mathrm{dep},l},
\zeta_l)$, together with a reject-all choice priced at the external rate
$\wext$. The floor may be reduced only through the user's voluntary, compensated agreement.

\paragraph{Over-charge offer and acceptance.} Independently of the
chosen level, the user may accept or decline a cost-free over-charge above the negotiated floor, recorded by $\accept{v}{k} \in \{0,1\}$; the banked energy is a no-cost buffer the driver may use before departure, and it carries over by \eqref{eq:carryover}. The formulation treats acceptance as binary and per-session; the main experiments assume every offer is accepted ($\accept{v}{k} \equiv 1$), stated explicitly, with the acceptance-rate sensitivity deferred to the behavioral model of Section~5.

\paragraph{Marginal contribution.} The compensation rests on two
components with different settlement times. The flexibility component is the ex ante expected reduction in building cost enabled by $v$'s negotiated choice, evaluated under the constraint set of Process~II (hence under $\FSL_d$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uflex}
\Uflex(\theta_v) \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\psihat}\Bigl[
J_d\bigl(\bm{\theta}_{\setminus v}\bigr) - J_d\bigl(\bm{\theta}_{\setminus v}, \theta_v\bigr) \Bigr],
\end{equation}
where $J_d(\cdot)$ is the day-$d$ building cost under $\pich$ and $\bm{\theta}_{\setminus v}$ collects the realized choices of other users and sampled future arrivals. The buffer component settles ex post: on a session overlapping an event window, it is the realized cost difference obtained by replaying the day with and without $v$'s surviving buffer,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ubuf}
\Ubuf(v,k) \;=\;
J^{\mathrm{re}}_d\bigl(\buff{d} \setminus \survived{v}{k}\bigr) - J^{\mathrm{re}}_d\bigl(\buff{d}\bigr),
\end{equation}
and $\Ubuf(v,k) = 0$ on sessions overlapping no event. The replay $J^{\mathrm{re}}_d$ is computed by re-solving the day-$d$ charging problem \eqref{eq:chobj} with the realized load, arrivals, choices, and event window all held fixed at their observed values, and with only the charging and discharging rates re-optimized; the two evaluations differ solely in whether $v$'s surviving buffer $\survived{v}{k}$ is available for discharge. Because the realized peak and event slack are recomputed under this re-optimization, and the building cost includes the degradation term $\Kbatt$, the difference \eqref{eq:ubuf} is the true marginal value of $v$'s buffer on that day, automatically net of battery wear; the user never bears wear separately. We use the per-user marginal value rather than a Shapley value: when several users' buffers shave the same peak, the marginal contributions need not sum to the total buffer value, so the building does not over-distribute, and each user is paid no more than the cost they demonstrably avoided. The resulting payments preserve the budget-feasibility guarantee (Section~4).

\paragraph{User cost.} User $v$'s cost for session $k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:usercost}
\ucost{k}{v} \;=\; \sum_{t \in \Tvk} \we{t}\,\gch{t}{v}
\;-\; \alpha\,\Uflex(\theta_v) \;-\; \Ubuf(v,k),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in [0,1]$ is the cost-sharing parameter on the flexibility component, and the buffer component is paid in full; a share parameter on $\Ubuf$ analogous to $\alpha$ is possible but fixed to one here. The banked excess itself is provided at no cost: $\banked{v}{k}$ does not appear in \eqref{eq:usercost}. Every term settles on realized quantities; no term depends on a forecast of user $v$'s behavior. The behavioral model mapping options to choice and acceptance probabilities (user types, inconvenience weights, and satisfaction) is presented in Section~5.

\subsection{Decomposed Objective and Information Structure}\label{sec:coupled}

\paragraph{Building objective.} Collecting the per-process contributions, the building minimizes the same net cost \eqref{eq:oracle} optimized by the oracle, now over the decomposed policies:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
\begin{aligned}
J = \mathbb{E}_{\psi}\Bigl[\, &\sum_{t \in \Tset} g_t + \wdr{}\,\pmax - \sum_{d} \Cinc{d} + \sum_{d} \Cpen{d} + \Ksoc \sum_{v,k} \zund{k}{v}\\ &+ \Kbatt \sum_{v,t} \qdis{t}{v} + \sum_{v,k} \bigl(\alpha\,\Uflex(\theta_v) + \Ubuf(v,k)\bigr)
\Bigr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Information structure.} Within day $d$, information flows
one way:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\FSL_d \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Process II constraint set}\\
\;\longrightarrow\; \text{Process III option generation};
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
the negotiation operates under the committed FSL and does not determine it, and we make no claim of within-day bidirectional coupling, which would render the formulation circular. Across days, the loop closes through the buffer and the forecasts:
\begin{equation*}
(\text{over-charge},\, o,\, \rho)\ \text{on day } d
\;\longrightarrow\; (\buffhat{d+1},\, \ohat,\, \rhohat)
\;\longrightarrow\; \FSL_{d+1}.
\end{equation*}
The persistence buffer expands the feasible commitment set
\eqref{eq:pledgeset}, formalized in Proposition~\ref{prop:monotone}; the
committed FSL gives the negotiation a concrete discharge target and an external revenue source. The building can extend a firm upstream guarantee because the committed quantity is selected after observing the buffer its users have already voluntarily accumulated.

\paragraph{Delivery risk.} The FSL is committed against $\psihat$. If
realized acceptance or yield falls below forecast, then $\buff{d} <
\buffhat{d}$ and the building is exposed to $\vio{t} > 0$ during events,
with the penalty borne entirely by the building and hedged by $(\lambda, \varepsilon)$ through \eqref{eq:pledgedepth}.

\paragraph{Solution space.} A feasible solution comprises:
\begin{itemize}
\item a non-anticipative commitment sequence $\{\FSL_d\}$;
\item an assignment $\assign_t$ mapping each arriving EV to a single
charger for its entire session;
\item per-step rates $\{\rate{t}{v}\}$ satisfying
\eqref{eq:socbounds}--\eqref{eq:load}, \eqref{eq:floor},
\eqref{eq:fslslack}, the charger limits, and the headroom rule;
\item negotiated agreements: for each session, an offered option set and
the user's selected $(\Eneg, \tdep, \zeta)$, with deviations within the chosen level's bounds;
\item over-charge acceptances $\{\accept{v}{k}\}$ and the induced buffer
accounting \eqref{eq:banked}--\eqref{eq:buffer},
\end{itemize}
jointly satisfying the participation and budget guarantees inherited from the negotiation mechanism (Section~4).
